Tens and ten more

Two minutes a day, no screen. Pick one. Stop while it is still fun.

The one idea: a ten is a thing you can hold. 27 is two tens and seven. Ten more is just one more ten. Every game below makes the ten visible before it becomes a word.

Games

  1. Cups of ten. Beans or pasta into cups, ten in each. Touch each cup: "ten, twenty, thirty." Then the loose ones: "thirty-one, thirty-two." Then add one more cup and ask for the new total without recounting.
  2. Dimes and pennies. Same game, more fun. "You have 27 cents. Here is a dime. Now?"
  3. The fridge chart. Point to any number. Ten more is the number straight below it. Three a day.
  4. Decade walks. Count up the stairs by ones and stop at 29, 39, 49: "What comes next?"
  5. Make ten. Hold up some fingers. She shows how many more make ten.
  6. Backwards from 20 while brushing teeth.

Mirror numbers

Normal until about seven. Put a small green dot where the numeral starts and have her trace from the dot. Praise the start, not the shape.

Ten more is one row down

27 is lit. Ten more, 37, is straight below it. Ask: "Where does 47 live?"

By the end of kindergarten

  • Counts to 100 by ones and by tens; counts on from any number.
  • Counts up to 20 things and knows the last number is how many.
  • Reads and writes 0 to 20. Compares two groups: more, fewer, same.
  • Adds and takes away within 10 with objects; knows the pairs within 5.
  • For any number 1 to 9, knows what makes 10. Sees 11 to 19 as a ten and some ones.
  • Compares length, weight and capacity; sorts and counts groups; names basic shapes.

"Ten more in your head" is a first-grade skill. Counting on her fingers at six is fine; these games grow the picture she will use instead.
